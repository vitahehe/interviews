## Contradiction

**Direct conflict between 'defined/precise' expectation and looser lab reality** (confidence: moderate)
In I01, student 2 anticipated the lab would require more precision and more clearly defined placement instructions, but student 1 later reports the actual experience was 'not as defined' as expected. This is a direct conflict between anticipated technical rigor (1.b) and the perceived reality of the lab's structure (2.a), suggesting a mismatch between how the lab was framed beforehand and how it was actually experienced.

**Perceived irrelevance of quantum topic (I01) versus perceived specialization usefulness (I02)** (confidence: moderate)
In I01, a student explicitly states the quantum lab topic is 'not really our area' since most of the group does astrophysics, framing the content as largely irrelevant to their studies. In contrast, I02 students frame the same type of lab content as a valuable 'introduction' for considering future specialization. This is a cross-interview contradiction in how perceived relevance/usefulness of the lab topic is experienced, though it may reflect different student populations/majors rather than a true inconsistency.

## Subtlety

**Prior optics background as a hidden prerequisite masking difficulty from the codebook's problem-solving frame** (confidence: moderate)
Reading the 1.c quotes in I01 together reveals a nuance not visible from any single quote: multiple students converge on the idea that their ability to 'solve' the lab's conceptual problems (understanding polarization, following the fast pre-lab explanation) depended entirely on having pre-existing optics coursework, not on the lab's own scaffolding. This suggests the lab's apparent problem-solving success may be an artifact of prior preparation rather than the lab's pedagogical design, a nuance that only emerges by comparing several students' accounts.
